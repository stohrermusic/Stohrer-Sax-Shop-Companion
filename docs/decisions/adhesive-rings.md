# Adhesive Rings: decisions and history

This file holds the why and the history behind the rules in the CLAUDE files, and the full detail they summarise: what was tried, measured, rejected and decided, with dates and the user's own words. The paragraphs were moved here verbatim from the CLAUDE files on 2026-11-10 (HEAD 01071f5); each section names where it came from. Add new decisions to the matching section, dated.

## Adhesive Rings (beta)

## Adhesive Rings (beta) (from CLAUDE-engines.md)

*Moved from CLAUDE-engines.md, section "Engines: Pad Generation, G-code, Tuner, Tooling > Adhesive Rings (beta)" (2026-11-10).*

Sizing Rules > **Lesser-used settings** > **Adhesive Rings (beta)** (Matt's spec, 2026-11-05). A tech who wraps pads in thin silicone sheet in place of leather has to stick the wrap to the back of the card, and the clean way is a ring of double-sided tape per pad. So the Pad Maker gains an opt-in fifth material, `adhesive_ring` ("Adhesive rings"): a plain ring, no engraving settings, no center-hole option, no darts, cut from tape. Off by default, and while off the material is not on the tab at all (see CLAUDE.md "Adhesive Rings").

### One geometry, four consumers

**One geometry, four consumers.** `svg_engine.adhesive_ring_geometry(pad_size, settings)` is the only place a ring is computed; the SVG renderer (`_render_svg_adhesive_rings`), the G-code engine (`_write_adhesive_ring_gcode`), the pad preview and the nesting preview all draw from it, and the nester reads its `footprint_r` through `get_disc_diameter`.

- *Outer (cut) diameter* = the card disc, `get_disc_diameter(pad, 'card', settings)`.

- *Ring width* = the back wrap the leather disc gets for that size, from the same call the leather disc uses (`leather_wrap_for_size`: base wrap, plus the dart `wrap_bonus` when the size has darts, times the wrap multiplier), plus `adhesive_ring_trim_mm`.

- *Inner diameter* = outer - 2 x width. **Pads of `ADHESIVE_RING_SOLID_MAX_PAD_MM` (9.5) and under are solid discs with no inner cut**, whatever the sizing rules make of the hole (Matt, 2026-11-05: "9.5 and under solid is good"; under his own preset those sizes would otherwise have had 3.3 to 5.7 mm holes). Above that size a hole under `ADHESIVE_RING_MIN_HOLE_MM` (2.0) is dropped too, a floor for unusual sizing rules. Both are constants with no UI. A trim can't thin the ring below `ADHESIVE_RING_MIN_WIDTH_MM` (0.5).

### Why the trim exists

**Why the trim exists.** The wrap figure is nominal, tuned on real pads, not a measured overlap on the card back: the card's thickness and its inset from the pad edge come out of it, and stretch puts some back. Silicone sheet is not leather either. So the computed wrap is a starting point and the user trims it (mm, either sign, default 0). **The dialog calls this number "Wrap clearance"**; the stored key stays `adhesive_ring_trim_mm` with the same meaning and sign (mm of ring width per side), the same precedent as the `dart_` keys behind "Darts". It is how far the ring's inner edge reaches past the edge of the wrap. Matt, 2026-11-05: "I think of negative clearance as being inside and hidden, positive clearance as being extra ring material", and "I want negative numbers to be reducing the ring size (increasing ID) and positive numbers to be increasing ring size (shrinking ID)". The control is a spinbox, 0.1 steps, -5.0 to 5.0, typing allowed; a typed value outside the range is held to it at Apply. The names tried and dropped on the way were "Ring width trim", "Inner edge clearance" and "Ring inner diameter adjustment" (that last one in diameter terms); no rename, migration or conversion of the key was ever shipped. Tk reformats and clamps a variable when a spinbox is created on it (a stored 0.25 becomes 0.2 just by opening the dialog), so the dialog puts the stored value back after building the control. Don't "fix" the ring by changing the wrap formula; that moves every leather disc.

### The number

**The number.** Every ring carries its pad size (`zone_label_text`, the same text the other labels use) as **stroke-font** polylines, never filled, whatever any `engraving_mode` says: `gcode_engine.get_text_strokes` at height 1, re-centred on the strokes' own box (the font cell has side bearings), then scaled. One constant decides where it goes, `ADHESIVE_RING_LEGIBLE_FONT_MM` (2.5):

- *Inside*: if the hole takes the number at 2.5 mm or more with `ADHESIVE_RING_HOLE_CLEARANCE_MM` (1.0) to the inner cut all round, it is centred in the hole, on the waste plug, at the largest height that fits, capped at `ADHESIVE_RING_MAX_FONT_MM` (5.0).

- *Outside*: otherwise (small rings, and every solid disc) it goes at 2.5 mm on the scrap, centred under the ring, its top `ADHESIVE_RING_OUTSIDE_GAP_MM` (1.0) below the outer cut.
A wide label needs a bigger hole than a narrow one, so "12.5" can sit outside while "12" and "13" sit inside. Measured 2026-11-05 for 7.0 to 60 mm in half sizes, with the 9.5 rule in place: 7.0 to 9.5 are solid discs under any sizing rules, so their number is always outside; beyond that the number is outside up to 11.5 and on 12.5, 13.5 and 14.5 under the program defaults, and on 10.5, 11.5 and 12.5 under `presets/Matt mode.json`. Matt agreed the 2.5 mm legible size and the outside list on 2026-11-05 (the list he saw had 9.0 inside; his 9.5 rule, given in the same reply, is what moves it out).

The strokes are stored Y-down relative to the ring centre ("under" is +y) and `adhesive_ring_number_strokes(geom, cx, cy, y_up=)` places them: as they are for SVG and canvases, mirrored in y for G-code, where `cy` is the already-flipped centre. That is what keeps the number under the ring, upright and unmirrored in both outputs; `test_engine_parity` and `test_adhesive_ring` pin it with the "7" glyph.

### Why the number is on the cut layer

**Why the number is on the cut layer.** The material has no engraving settings (Matt: a plain ring), so there is nothing to engrave it with. In the SVG the number shares the ring's one layer colour (`layer_colors['adhesive_ring_cut']`, LightBurn 07 Magenta) and is emitted as polylines rather than `<text>`, so LightBurn gets single lines. In the G-code it is traced at the ring's cut speed, power, passes and air (layer comment `C07`, with a line in the file saying so). Order per ring is number, inner circle, outer circle, so an inside number is traced while its plug is still attached: with "by layer" grouping that is all numbers, all inner cuts, all outer cuts; with "by pad" the three steps ring by ring. Kerf goes inward on the inner cut and outward on the outer; none on the number. Both cuts are linearized with `ADHESIVE_RING_CUT_SEGMENTS` (72): the inner cut is nearly as big as the outer, not a 3 mm center hole, and at the center-hole figure (36) a 51 mm pad's inner cut had 3.7 mm flats, 0.08 mm off a true circle (found by the independent test pass, 2026-11-05). The SVG is true circles either way. The number gets the full cut passes, and all of its passes come first, before any cut, the way engraving does (Matt, 2026-11-05).

### Footprint versus cut radius (the trap)

**Footprint versus cut radius (the trap).** An outside number needs sheet area no neighbour may take and that is on material. That is done by handing the nester a larger disc, never by adding obstacles and never by touching the parity-pinned scan functions: `get_disc_diameter(pad, 'adhesive_ring', settings)` returns the **nesting footprint**, the circle centred on the ring that encloses the ring and its number's box (equal to the cut when the number is inside). The nester's usual 1 mm spacing then keeps every number clear of every other ring and inside the sheet or polygon. The consequence: **for this material the radius stored in `placed` is the footprint, not the cut**. Every renderer must take the cut radius from `adhesive_ring_geometry`; one that trusts `placed` cuts a circle up to twice the size (12.5 nests as 17.1 mm and cuts at 9.75 under the defaults). The nesting preview is passed `settings=` for the same reason and draws the real ring and number; without settings it falls back to footprint circles. The footprint is a full circle round a number that only sits on one side, so small rings cost more tape than their size suggests; rings nest as solid discs (no ring-inside-ring nesting in this version).

### What stands down

**What stands down.** Labeled Zones (`nest_with_zones` nests rings plainly; a zone's border and label are an engraving layer this material doesn't have, and `_prepare_generation` skips the zones/`max` refusal for a rings-only job), the label placement and auto-fit code, the locator marks, the center hole, and `check_for_oversized_engravings`.

### Settings

**Settings.** `adhesive_ring_enabled` and `adhesive_ring_trim_mm` are in `DEFAULT_SETTINGS` and `SIZING_PRESET_KEYS` (backfilled by `normalize_sizing_preset`, so older presets still name themselves). `gcode_settings['adhesive_ring']` is cut-only (`config.GCODE_CUT_ONLY_MATERIALS` / `GCODE_CUT_ONLY_KEYS`): cut speed, power, passes, kerf, cut air. `GcodeSettingsWindow` builds one cut row and the kerf for it and branches on the same tuple when capturing, applying, saving and resetting. **The default laser values are a starting point, not tested values** (1500 mm/min, 45 %, 1 pass, air on, kerf 0.2 = the card default; Matt, 2026-11-05). Nobody has cut this with the app and no published figure fits, so they are a straight scale from the one diode data point found (a 20 W diode cutting 1.5-3 mm silicone sheet at 60-75 %, 200-300 mm/min) down to a 0.5 mm (0.020 in) sheet on a 40 W diode: about 0.72 J per mm of cut, 18 W at 25 mm/s. No margin on energy, deliberately (Matt: "we don't want margin on energy, that could roast it"): a cut that doesn't go through can be repeated, a roasted part can't. An earlier 1200 mm/min at 70 % proposal carried a threefold margin and was rejected for that reason. The G-code section's note and the user guide call the defaults a starting point and send the user to Tooling > Speed & Power Test; no text may say the value was tested. The existing Default-preset backfill in `main.py` gives current users an `adhesive_ring` Default. `laser_framing_power_by_material['adhesive_ring']` defaults to 10; the Framing Power dialog shows its row only while the feature is on and merges into the stored map so a hidden row keeps its value.

### Machine control

**Machine control.** Nothing special was needed: `on_frame_and_cut` takes any single checked material other than `exact_size`, `_get_material_dimensions` gives rings the sheet or the custom polygon (the paper-size option is card's alone), and the ring writer runs after the shared Y flip, so `flip_height_mm` (camera-captured polygons) applies. Verified with a mock sender in `test_adhesive_ring`: the streamed cut is the exported file line for line, in standard, polygon and scrap mode, and the framing pass uses the ring's own power with M4.

Tests, three suites:

- `tools/test_adhesive_ring.py` (51): geometry, the number rule, the Wrap clearance counter (spinbox, range, step, tooltip, +1.0 / -1.0 in the written SVG, every tenth through Apply, save and reopen, typed nonsense), nesting, both outputs, presets, the UI appearing only while enabled, both previews, and Frame & Cut against a mock sender (standard, camera polygon with a separate outline, scrap mode, scrap mode on a camera polygon with a stopped cut then two scraps in a row).

- `tools/test_adhesive_ring_fuzz.py`: 160 seeded random jobs (pad lists from 7.0 to 60 in half sizes, program defaults / Matt mode / random universal and per-range sizing, darts universal / per-range / off, trims of both signs, kerf, grouping, passes, compatibility mode, every edge bias, rectangles and convex / concave / tilted polygons, Frame & Cut's flip-height override on half the polygons) plus all 107 half sizes under `presets/Matt mode.json`. It reads the **written SVG and G-code files** back and checks each ring against expectations worked out from the sizing rules alone, not from the helper under test: cut = card, inner = wrap + trim, the number is that ring's size in the stroke font and upright, inside or outside by the rule, nothing touching anything else, everything on material, SVG = G-code after the flip, order and laser-off. `python tools/test_adhesive_ring_fuzz.py 400 7` runs more jobs on another seed.

- `tools/test_adhesive_ring_upgrade.py` (14): a config folder as v2.9 left it (the stripped defaults were compared with v2.9's `config.py`: identical), with a user's own values and Matt mode applied, driven through the real app and the real dialogs. The folder loads without an error, the user's values and presets are untouched, the new material gets its Default G-code preset on disk, "Matt mode" and the active G-code presets are still detected; the feature is switched on in Sizing Rules, the ring ticked, the feature switched off again (all four pieces of UI go, nothing is generated from the stale tick by either Generate button or Frame & Cut, the hidden Layer Colors / Framing Power values survive a save); `python main.py --selftest` on the old folder and with the feature on; and `run_tour` as a gate with the feature on and off (the tour's G-code stop shows the ring section while it is on). What the tour's Sizing Rules stop showed is sampled when the tour closes that dialog (the suite wraps `top.destroy`), never on a timer of the test's own: `run_tour` schedules each stop's finish before it opens the stop, so a dialog that takes longer to build than `step_ms` is closed the moment it exists. The first version sampled with `after(250)` against a 350 ms step and lost that race on CI's Intel Mac (`0889d94`: the OFF gate failed, the ON gate happened to pass, and `build` was skipped). The suite now sleeps 0.45 s inside the wrapped dialog on purpose, longer than a step, so the gate proves it does not depend on machine speed; with the same sleep the old probe fails both gates.

Each gate was run against a deliberately broken copy of the code: wrong outer disc, no dart bonus, trim ignored, no solid-disc cut-off, the 9.5 rule dropped or off by half a size, number above / upside down / unflipped / filled / saying another size, footprint drawn as the cut, zones applied, UI shown while off, the material only hidden instead of removed, framing at another material's power, and so on. The gates that let a break through the first time (hole clearance, the oversized-engraving skip, glyph orientation in the fuzz checker) were tightened until they failed too. The four classic materials' SVG and G-code were also hashed against HEAD over 48 cases and came out identical. Not covered by anything automated: a real cut on a real laser. Extended: `test_pad_maker_e2e`, `test_svg_wellformed`, `test_tooltips`, `test_gcode_presets_workflow`, `test_sizing_presets_workflow`, `test_framing_power`, `test_engine_parity`.
